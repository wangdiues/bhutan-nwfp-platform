Non-Wood Forest Products of Bhutan

Conservation-first management, rural livelihoods, and green value chains.

Bhutan's NWFP sector has considerable potential, but sustainable development depends on stronger inventories, technical capacity, processing infrastructure, certification, and market access.

Top Dzongkhags

Legal Status of NWFPs in Bhutan

Forest Policy, 2011

Bhutan's forest policy places conservation above all other considerations. Harvesting or commercial use of the forest is only permitted after conservation goals have been met and only if management policies ensure sustainable use.

Current governance

The use and management of NWFPs in Bhutan are primarily governed by the Forest and Nature Conservation Act of Bhutan 2023.

Planning and Institutional Roles

DFOs, Park Offices, and FRPMD

The concerned DFOs and Park Offices are responsible for facilitating NWFP allocation and utilization and assisting community-based forest enterprise establishment through planning and monitoring of operations. Forest Resource Planning & Management Division (FRPMD) oversees NWFP management and provides technical support for the preparation and implementation of NWFP plans.

Under the Conservation Act, all NWFP harvesting activities must be formally integrated into either PA, Community Forest Management Plans (CFMPs), or FMU plans in compliance with the technical guidelines provided by DoFPS.

Problems and Constraints

Developing Non-Wood Forest Products

Knowledge and management gaps

Too little is known about existing NWFP resources, management strategies, best harvesting practices, and marketing opportunities. Professionals in the sector remain few, and institutional support is weak.

Inventory and financial constraints

There is an acute lack of inventory data. Identifying resources and their locations requires funding, while public financial resources must cover many competing development needs.

Training and enforcement gaps

The shortage of quality training institutes limits development. Forestry laws and codes remain inadequate in some areas, and comprehensive enforcement would require more field officers.

Mountain geography

Bhutan's rugged Eastern Himalayan terrain makes road construction expensive and transport slow, costly, and sometimes dangerous.

Seasonal access barriers

During rainy seasons and winters, roads can become impassable from landslides, washouts, ice, and snow, creating a major obstacle to development.

Sector-wide impact

These constraints affect social and economic advancement, including Bhutan's emerging non-wood forest products industry.

Long-Term Activities

General Recommendations

  • Establish national priorities based on all known NWFPs and rank them using agreed criteria.
  • Build a more complete inventory of priority commodities, including species, locations, and quantities.
  • Train personnel in plant identification, collection methods, processing, manufacturing, quality control, and design.
  • Maintain gene banks, tissue culture, seed storage, voucher herbarium specimens, and extension centres.
  • Set up post-harvest facilities close to commodity sources for drying, sifting, deep freezing, and controlled atmosphere handling.
  • Initiate scholarships and grants for related degree programmes and short-term technology training.
  • Create a national clearing house for NWFP matters, including marketing and independent annual reviews.

Commercialization and Value Chains

Current Market Reality

Despite the potential of NWFPs to contribute to rural livelihoods and green economic development, their current use in Bhutan remains largely at subsistence levels, with minimal commercialization.

Today, Cordyceps sinensis represents the most economically significant NWFP in Bhutan, accounting for the bulk of NWFP revenue despite the diverse variety of NWFPs available in the country.

Monetization constraints

  • Fragmented value chains
  • Lack of processing facilities
  • Absence of product certification or quality assurance standards

Export and Enterprise Challenges

Why NWFP exports remain limited

Limited value addition

Many NWFPs, including bamboo and mushrooms, are sold raw at local markets or auction yards without branding or processing.

Weak market access

Limited certification makes it difficult for producers to access premium markets or meet export standards.

Capability gaps

NWFP enterprises often lack technical, financial, and digital capabilities, including business management, packaging, and production skills.

Limited scalability

Seasonal availability and weather fluctuations make raw material supply unreliable, limiting consistent large-scale operations.
