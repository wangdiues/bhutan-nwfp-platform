Legal Status

Forest Policy, 2011 and Conservation Act, 2023

Bhutan's forest policy places conservation above all other considerations. Harvesting or commercial use of forests is permitted only after conservation goals have been met and only when management policies ensure sustainable use.

The use and management of NWFPs are primarily governed by the Forest and Nature Conservation Act of Bhutan 2023. Harvesting activities must be integrated into PA, CFMP, or FMU plans in compliance with DoFPS technical guidelines.

Institutional Roles

DFOs, Park Offices, and FRPMD

DFOs and Park Offices facilitate NWFP allocation and utilization, assist community-based forest enterprise establishment, and monitor operations. FRPMD oversees NWFP management and provides technical support for preparing and implementing NWFP plans.

Problems and Constraints

Why NWFP development remains difficult

Resource knowledge gaps

Too little is known about resources, management strategies, harvesting practices, and markets.

Weak inventory data

Species, locations, and quantities remain insufficiently documented.

Limited training

Technical, processing, management, and enterprise capabilities need investment.

Mountain logistics

Rugged terrain, narrow roads, rainfall, landslides, ice, and snow limit reliable transport.

Market Reality

Cordyceps dominates revenue

Cordyceps sinensis is the most economically significant NWFP in Bhutan and accounts for the bulk of NWFP revenue, despite the country's broader NWFP diversity.

  • Fragmented value chains
  • Lack of processing facilities
  • Limited certification and quality assurance

Long-Term Priorities

Recommendations for developing NWFPs

Prioritize commodities

Establish national priorities using agreed criteria.

Complete inventories

Document species, locations, and quantities.

Train personnel

Improve collection, processing, manufacturing, quality control, and design skills.

Build markets

Create clearing-house functions, certification pathways, and annual review mechanisms.
