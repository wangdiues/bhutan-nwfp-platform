Legal status

Forest Policy 2011 & Conservation Act 2023

Bhutan's forest policy places conservation above all other considerations. Harvesting or commercial use of forests is permitted only after conservation goals have been met and only when management policies ensure sustainable use.

The use and management of NWFPs are primarily governed by the Forest and Nature Conservation Act of Bhutan 2023. Harvesting activities must be integrated into PA, CFMP, or FMU plans in compliance with DoFPS technical guidelines.

Institutional roles

DFOs, Park Offices & FRPMD

DFOs and Park Offices facilitate NWFP allocation and utilization, assist community-based forest enterprise establishment, and monitor operations. FRPMD oversees NWFP management and provides technical support for preparing and implementing NWFP plans.

Planning PA, CFMP, and FMU integration
Monitoring Operations and allocation oversight

Problems & constraints

Why NWFP development remains difficult

Resource knowledge gaps

Too little is known about resources, management strategies, harvesting practices, and market linkages.

Weak inventory data

Species, locations, and quantities remain insufficiently documented across the country.

Limited training

Technical, processing, management, and enterprise capabilities need sustained investment.

Mountain logistics

Rugged terrain, narrow roads, rainfall, landslides, ice, and snow limit reliable year-round transport.

Market reality

Cordyceps dominates revenue

Cordyceps sinensis is the most economically significant NWFP in Bhutan and accounts for the bulk of NWFP revenue, despite the country's broader species diversity.

  • Fragmented value chains with few processing facilities
  • Limited certification and quality assurance infrastructure
  • Underdeveloped buyer linkages and market access pathways

Long-term priorities

Recommendations for developing NWFPs

Prioritise key commodities

Establish national priorities using agreed criteria that weigh conservation, livelihoods, and market viability.

Complete resource inventories

Systematically document species, locations, densities, and quantities across all forest types.

Train field personnel and collectors

Improve collection, processing, manufacturing, quality control, and enterprise design skills at community level.

Build market systems

Create clearing-house functions, certification pathways, traceability frameworks, and annual review mechanisms.
